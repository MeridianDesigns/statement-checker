How to read your Etsy Payment-account statement (2026)

A free guide for Etsy sellers. Facts checked October 27, 2026.

Etsy's monthly statement is the complete record of your money: every sale, fee, ad charge, refund, tax pass-through and bank transfer, one row each. It is also confusing. Fees show -- where you expect numbers, deposits hide their amount in a sentence, and one order can produce eighteen rows. This guide explains each column and row type, and how to get from a month of rows to what you kept.

Samples come from real US, euro and UK statements, with order numbers replaced by #1234567890.

1. Where the statement lives

To get it: Shop Manager, then Finances, then Monthly statements, then View all monthly statements. Pick the year and month and choose Generate CSV. Etsy emails you a link ("Take me to download"); open it and choose Download CSV. You get one month per file, and generation is not instant.

Filename: etsy_statement_YYYY_M.csv, with no leading zero on the month: September 2026 is etsy_statement_2026_9.csv. The file begins with a UTF-8 byte-order mark.

Why not use the screen? Recent activities covers only the last 60 days.

The three balances (September 2026 redesign). Your Payment account now shows:

  • Current: the balance your deposits are based on.
  • Pending: money from sales Etsy has not released to you yet.
  • Total: Current and Pending together.

Etsy's help article on the Payment account has the rules: https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015747228.

2. The columns, one by one

Column What it holds
Date The day the row posted, as text.
Type Sale, Fee, Marketing, Shipping, Tax, VAT, Refund, Deposit, Payment or Adjustment.
Title What the row is. Often carries the order number or an item name.
Info A reference: Order #…, Listing #…, a card, or (Etsy Ads) the date of the clicks. Blank on Sale and Refund rows.
Currency Your payment-account currency.
Amount Gross money on Sale and Refund rows. -- everywhere else.
Fees & Taxes The money on every other row. Negative is a cost, positive is a credit. -- on Sale rows.
Net What the row did to your balance. Equals Amount on Sale and Refund rows, Fees & Taxes on the rest.
Tax Details -- in every real statement we examined.
Status, Availability Date New in the September 2026 layout (below).

Three things that trip people

  1. -- means "not here," not zero. Every non-Sale row has -- in Amount and its value in Fees & Taxes. Sum Amount and you miss every fee. Sum Net.
  2. Money is text with symbols: $6.78, -$0.45, €28.68, -€0.17, £1,208.35. Strip the symbol and commas, treat a leading minus as negative, treat -- as empty.
  3. Dates are text, and the format depends on the shop. US: July 24, 2026. The UK file we examined: 16 January, 2024, with a zero-padded day. Parse them yourself.

Status and Availability Date. The July, August and September 2026 US files we examined have 11 header names: the usual nine plus these two. In all three, the data rows still hold only nine values, so neither column has anything we can read. What Etsy writes there is unverified. Older files had 9 columns (euro 2025, US April 2026) or 8 with no Tax Details (the UK file from 2024, tab-separated). Match columns by name, not position.

Rows run newest first, and within an order the fee rows sit above the Sale row.

3. Every row Type, and what the Title tells you

Class is how to treat the row: income, cost, credit (a cost reversed), pass-through (tax Etsy collects and remits; not yours, not a fee) or movement (money moving, neither income nor cost).

Rarer rows (third-party sources, wording unverified): auto-renew listing variants, Buyer Fee, and a one-time shop set-up fee with a credit under Type Payment. Review any Title you do not recognize.

Order number: Info or Title? Sale and Refund rows carry it in the Title (Info is blank). Fee, Offsite, Tax and Etsy-credit rows carry it in Info. VAT rows are unreliable: Info may say Order #…, order: …, a transaction number, or nothing, so some match an order only by sitting beside it in the file.

Do not assume every order has a "Sales tax paid by buyer" row. Only 8 of the 59 orders in our UK file do.

5. Why your deposit isn't your profit

A deposit is money Etsy moved to your bank from your Current balance. It does not measure a month:

  1. It batches days. One deposit covers all net activity since the last one, often across two months.
  2. It leaves out Pending. Sales not yet released are not in Current, so not in the deposit.
  3. It comes before your own costs. Materials, postage bought elsewhere and software are not on this statement.
  4. Refunds and returns lag. A refund and its credits can post weeks after the sale, and a bank can send a deposit back. In the September 2026 file, $0.33 sent to your bank account on September 8 was followed by Returned deposit, +$0.33, on September 11.

How to tie a deposit to activity. Sort the file oldest to newest and keep a running total of Net. At each Deposit row, the running total is that deposit; then reset to zero. In our euro file all three January 2025 deposits tie to the cent:

Deposit Net since the previous deposit
€19.96 sent to your bank account (Jan 6) €19.96
€99.30 sent to your bank account (Jan 24) €99.30
€20.51 sent to your bank account (Jan 30) €20.51

The first deposit of a month needs the previous month's tail; subtract still-pending sales before comparing.

7. What changed in 2024 to 2026, and what will change again

  • September 2024: a one-time shop set-up fee, per a third-party report (unverified).
  • 2025 to 2026: the titles Tax: Transaction and Listing fee ($0.20 USD) (non-USD shops) appear.
  • February 13 and October 5, 2026: fee-policy revisions with no rate change.
  • June 22, 2026: Regulatory Operating fee rates changed in seven countries; Hungary added.
  • September 2026: the Payment account moved to Current, Pending and Total balances, and US statement headers grew from 9 names to 11.

What to re-check: the fee policy page and the regulatory operating fee help article. Rates change more often than the file layout, and the layout changes without notice. Anything that hard-codes column positions or title text breaks first.

8. What a spreadsheet has to do to read this file

  1. Classify by Type plus Title, not Type alone. A Fee row can be a cost or a credit, a Tax row a pass-through or a cost, and a Deposit's amount lives in its Title.
  2. Treat credits as negative fees. Positive Fees & Taxes on any Credit for… or VAT: … credit row reduces your fee total.
  3. Keep pass-through tax out of fees. Sales tax paid by buyer and Refund to buyer for sales tax offset tax inside the Sale gross; revenue ex-tax is the Sale plus that negative row.
  4. Parse deposits from the Title, and subtract Returned deposit.
  5. Match columns by header name, not position: files have had 8, 9 and 11 headers, and rows can be shorter than the header.
  6. Read UTF-8 with a BOM, keep order numbers as text, and parse each date format yourself.
  7. Group fees by the text before the colon, not the full Title, because item names are embedded.
